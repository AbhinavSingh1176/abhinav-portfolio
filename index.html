02CompleteApr 2025 – Jan 2026

Micro Pin-Fin Heatsink Simulation

Result ~35% faster simulation turnaround with accuracy preserved

Undergraduate research at Purdue's Cooling Technologies Research Center, characterizing micro pin-fin heatsinks for high heat flux electronics cooling. I built the simulation pipeline end to end and showed that cheaper models could stand in for full 3D runs.

ProblemFull 3D conjugate heat transfer models were accurate but expensive to run across pin-fin designs.
RoleOwned geometry, meshing, solver setup, and post-processing in ANSYS Fluent and Mechanical.
ApproachMesh-resolution studies; reduced-order 2D and symmetry models validated against full 3D baselines for heat transfer and pressure drop.
ResultTurnaround cut by roughly 35% at preserved accuracy. Methods documented for the lab's next researchers.

03CompleteJan 2025 – May 2026

CVT Mount & Powertrain Hardware, ASME Racing

Result Purdue ASME Racing's first in-house CVT mount, proven on track

Chassis flex and CVT belt wear were costing the team pit time every race. I designed a new CVT mount and the hardware around it, then fabricated and tested it with the team through the season.

ProblemChassis flex and severe belt wear forced manual belt changes at pit stops.
RolePowertrains member; owned the CVT mount design.
ApproachAutodesk Fusion CAD with packaging, weight, and cost trades negotiated with the chassis and engine subteams. 3D-printed prototypes, then sheet metal fixtures, drivetrain shrouds, and adjustable exhaust mounts made by manual machining.
ResultMount ran on track across the season and the belt problem was contained. Root cause analysis on heat-related failures drove the shroud design.

About

I grew up on maths and physics problems and competed in olympiads, then came to Purdue wanting problems with real consequences. What keeps me in mechanical engineering is the point where a model and a measurement finally agree, and the work it takes to get there: checking boundary conditions, instrumenting the test, and trusting the data over the plan.

That has taken me from CFD at the Cooling Technologies Research Center to engine test cells at Cummins. Next, I want to work on thermal systems and hardware validation, where moving heat is the constraint that decides whether a product works.

Outside work: the gym, Formula 1, and chess.
